The simple caution-only rule used in this sample

A caution flag is raised only when a tightly mapped official series shows stress:

  • Input cost: the mapped commodity is up 20% or more in three months.
  • Factory output: the mapped IIP (Index of Industrial Production) series is down 5% or more year on year in the latest month.
  • Listed peers: at least two tightly mapped peers have results, and their median sales are down 5% or more year on year, or their median operating margin is down 2 percentage points or more. One peer is shown for context but does not raise a flag.

One flag can move OK to lend → Caution. Two independent flags can move Caution → Avoid. A flag never creates a score where the industry is not scored. Improving figures never move a stance toward OK, until this layer has been back-tested. This is the same one-way idea as Lending Radar’s rating-actions layer.

  • Export receivable days and the share of a few buyers.
  • Inventory days of raw material and finished goods.

Data gaps, back-testing, and rollout

Where the map is missing or weak

  • Ceramic-only exports (HS 69) are not available separately. PTA and MEG prices are not in the commodity file. Dye-chain feedstock prices (benzene and similar) are not in the file.
  • Most listed tile makers and Surat polyester names are not in the Nifty 500 results extract. Somany and Filatex already appear on the live Lending Radar card from its own filing store, but they are absent from Smart Money fundamentals.json.
  • IIP, trade and commodity series are national, not Morbi-, Surat- or Vapi-only. Capacity utilisation (RBI OBICUS) is manufacturing-wide, so it is not used as a cluster line.
  • Company results in Smart Money start with the quarter ended Mar 2025, so year-on-year growth only begins with Mar 2026 — about three comparable quarters.

What a back-test would need before this layer could ever lift a stance toward OK

The same official series would be lined up against later stress: RBI Financial Stability Report sector bad-loan (GNPA) ratios, and rating-agency upgrades versus downgrades, the way Lending Radar already uses those sources. IIP and IMF commodity prices in the file go back about two to three years; trade figures go back to FY 2018-19. Listed-company results are too short on their own. Until that test is done, the rule stays one-way: it may add caution, never comfort.

Effort to roll this out to all clusters

Lending Radar already has sourced local write-ups for a few dozen city × industry cards, and many more scored pairs without a local write-up. A rollout would need a simple mapping table for each industry: which IIP series, which trade chapters, which commodity, and which listed peers count as tight. Many cards would stay grey on at least one of those legs, as this sample does for PTA and HS 69. The engineering work is modest if the new lines are added only to Why, with the caution-only flag, and the five-section layout is left alone. The slower work is agreeing the maps, keeping them tight, and running the back-test before any upward move is allowed.

Ankleshwar chemicals would use the same lines as Vapi. Other fair next clusters, if the maps stay tight, would be Ahmedabad cotton textiles (cotton prices plus the textiles IIP) and Dahej petrochemicals (Brent and, with a clearer feedstock map, LNG).
